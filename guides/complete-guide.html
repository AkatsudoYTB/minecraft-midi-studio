Step 1 — Installation

Goal: download Minecraft MIDI Studio and start it for the first time (about 5 minutes).

What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 computer (64-bit).
  • An internet connection at the first start (about 41 MB, only once: see below).
  • Minecraft Java Edition, to play the music in your world. It does not need to be started before: the software finds the worlds of the official launcher, Modrinth, CurseForge and Prism Launcher by itself.
  • About 350 MB of free disk space (program, official sounds, sample cache).

Note Block Studio is not needed. If it is installed, the full edition can also use its custom sounds.

Download

  1. Open the Download page of the website (GitHub Releases).
  2. Download MinecraftMidiStudio-<version>-windows.exe.
  3. Put it in a folder of your choice, for example Documents\Minecraft MIDI Studio.

First start

  1. Double-click MinecraftMidiStudio.exe.
  2. Windows may show “Windows protected your PC” (the program is not signed with a paid certificate). Click More info, then Run anyway. This only happens the first time.
  3. The first start takes a few seconds longer: the program unpacks itself once into your cache folder.
  4. The Home page opens.

The official Minecraft sounds

The instrument and mob sounds, and the textures of the icons, belong to Mojang: they are not inside the program. The first time they are needed (opening the catalog or a project):

  • if Minecraft 1.21.1 is installed on the computer, the software uses its files;
  • otherwise it downloads them itself from Mojang's official servers, like a launcher: about 41 MB, only once, every file being checked against the fingerprint published by Mojang.

The progress is shown at the bottom of the window. Without internet, the software says so; the Download now button of the Settings page starts the download again later.

Uninstall

Delete MinecraftMidiStudio.exe. To also remove your settings, the downloaded sounds, the cache and the backups, delete the two folders above. Nothing else is installed on your computer.

Step 2 — First settings

Goal: choose the language and check where the sounds come from (usually nothing to change).

Open the Settings page from the left menu.

Language

  1. In Language, choose English or Français.
  2. Click Restart now (or simply restart the program later).

The language is saved. It is also used for the messages of the datapack (in-game chat) and the description of the resource pack generated afterwards.

Official Minecraft sounds

Usually nothing to do: the software uses the sounds of your Minecraft, or downloads them itself from Mojang's official servers (see step 1). This section shows the version used and lets you:

  • Download now — start the download again (after an internet cut, for example);
  • Delete the downloaded files — free about 41 MB (they will be downloaded again when needed);
  • untick the automatic download if you prefer to use only your Minecraft installation.

Sound and game sources

  • Note Block Studio sounds (optional) — only to add its custom sounds to the full edition. Usually detected automatically (…\Minecraft Note Block Studio\Data\Sounds); if not, Browse….
  • Minecraft folder — leave it on Automatic if you use the official launcher. Modrinth, CurseForge and Prism Launcher instances are found by themselves.
  • Added instances — only if your game folder is somewhere unusual: click Add… and choose the folder that contains saves.
  • World backups — where the ZIP backups made before installing are stored.

Timeline and audio

  • The colours of the timeline (blue = silence, green = notes playing) can be changed.
  • Audio output and Preview volume only affect the preview inside the software, not the game.

Step 3 — Choose a world (wizard step 1)

Goal: pick the Minecraft world where the music structure will be placed.

  1. On the Home page, click New project. The Wizard opens on step 1, World.
  2. The list shows every world found (official launcher, Modrinth, CurseForge, Prism). A world you create while the program is open appears by itself; Refresh the list forces a new search.
  3. Click a world: its version, game mode, commands and folder are shown below.
  4. Click Use this world.

Good to know

  • Commands must be allowed in the world (the music is controlled with /function). If they are not: in game, Open to LAN → Allow commands, or use a server.
  • Close the world in Minecraft before installing the music (step 10). The software checks it.
  • Create a ZIP backup… copies the whole world into your backup folder without changing it. A backup is also offered automatically before any installation.
  • Nothing is written into the world at this step.

Step 4 — Import a MIDI file (wizard step 2)

Goal: load the song you want to hear in Minecraft.

  1. Click Next to reach step 2, MIDI.
  2. Drag a .mid / .midi file onto the drop zone, or click Choose a MIDI file….
  3. The analysis runs in the background: tempo and tempo changes, duration, tracks, channels, instruments, notes, chords and percussion.

What you see

  • File information — size, format, resolution, duration, initial tempo, time signature, key, number of tracks and notes.
  • Tempo changes — the table on the right lists every change (they are respected in game).
  • Warnings — for example tracks without a name, notes without an end, or a percussion track on a melodic channel.

A copy of the MIDI file is stored inside the project, so the project still works if you move or delete the original file.

Free edition: only the first 20 seconds of the song are played and converted. The full edition converts the whole song.

Step 5 — Choose the tracks (wizard step 3)

Goal: keep only the tracks you want in Minecraft, and listen to them.

  1. Tick the tracks to convert. Buttons: Select all, Select none, Invert.
  2. Click Play (or press Space) to hear the ticked tracks.
  3. Click the ▶ button at the start of a row to hear that track alone, without changing the ticks. Click ■ to stop. The coloured bar then shows the notes of that track only.

The timeline

  • Blue: no note is playing. Green: at least one note is playing.
  • The lighter veil shows the part already played.
  • Click the bar to jump anywhere; ← / → move by 5 seconds.
  • Per-track view shows one row per ticked track.

The sound stops by itself when you go to another step.

The sound here comes from a small built-in synthesizer: it is only there to recognise the song. The real Minecraft sound is heard in step 5, Preview.
Free edition: up to 3 ticked tracks and 300 played notes.

Step 6 — Instruments and drums (wizard step 4)

Goal: give each track a Minecraft sound.

  1. Each track automatically gets a sound from its General MIDI instrument number, with a fixed table of the 128 instruments, like Note Block Studio's MIDI import (see the table at the bottom of this guide): for example no. 1 piano → harp, no. 33 to 40 (basses) → double bass, no. 41 violin → flute. The track name and its notes do not matter. The reason is written under the sound.
  2. To change it: choose another sound in the catalog on the right (search box, categories, Listen), then click Assign the selected sound to the track.
  3. Adjust the Relative volume and the Transposition (in semitones) of the track.
  4. Lowest note / Highest note let you hear the extremes of the track with the chosen sound.

Drum tracks

A percussion track uses a key → sound table: each drum key (kick, snare, hi-hat…) is linked to a sound and a transposition, the way Note Block Studio creators do it. Keys without a sound can be ignored or played with the snare drum. Restore the default table undoes your changes.

Sounds available

  • Note blocks (official Minecraft files: those of your installation, or downloaded from Mojang).
  • Mob sounds of the selected Minecraft version, sorted by category (voice, hurt, movement…).
  • Custom sounds placed in the Note Block Studio Custom folder (full edition, if Note Block Studio is installed).
Free edition: 8 basic note block instruments (no pling, bell, chime, flute, guitar, xylophones or bit), and only the voices of 3 farm animals for mob sounds.

Step 7 — Preview and samples (wizard steps 5 and 6)

Goal: hear the real Minecraft result, then prepare all the audio files.

Preview (step 5)

  1. The samples needed for the preview are prepared automatically (the first time takes a few seconds).
  2. Converted (Minecraft samples) plays the song as the game will: notes aligned on 50 ms ticks, each sound played in full, 8 velocity levels.
  3. Original MIDI switches back to the original at the same position, to compare.
  4. Listen to lets you hear one track only.

Exporting the audio (full edition)

Export the audio… saves the song as a file, for example for the soundtrack of a video:

  • Sound: Minecraft (the real samples, as in the game) or Original MIDI;
  • Tracks: all the selected tracks mixed, or a single one;
  • Format: MP3, WAV, FLAC or OGG.

The file is at least as long as the song, which makes it easy to sync with a video.

Samples (step 6)

One audio file is prepared for each note actually played, so Minecraft never has to change the pitch of a sound (that is what makes the result clean).

  • Margin for /pitch — extra notes so that the in-game /function music:<name>/pitch command always finds a sample.
  • Notes outside an instrument's range — moved to the nearest octave, skipped, or generated anyway.
  • Maximum length of a sample — long sounds are shortened with a fade.
  • Normalisation — same perceived volume for every note of an instrument.
  • Method per instrument — resampling (classic note block sound) or pitch only (keeps the duration, better for voices).

Click Generate the samples. Samples already made with the same settings are reused from the cache.

Step 8 — The structure (wizard step 7)

Goal: choose how the music looks in the world.

What gets built

  • Powered rails side by side, lit by redstone torches under the floor.
  • The minecarts always appear at the far end of the rails and travel towards you. In the lava they disappear: new minecarts appear at the far end for the next notes.
  • Above the trigger line: an arch of command blocks and a coloured block that lights up when the note plays (one colour per track).
  • After the line, the minecarts fall into a lava pit.
  • At the front, on your side: a button console (play, pause, stop, volume −/+, tempo −/+, pitch −/+) with signs.

The sound and the flashes are triggered by the datapack's sequencer: the minecarts are only visual, so the music stays in time even if the server slows down.

Layout settings

  • Lanes — one per track (compact), one per played note (piano roll) or one per pitch.
  • Gap between rails — 1 = rails right next to each other.
  • Rail length and Minecart speed — they decide how long before its note a minecart appears.
  • Wait before the start — after “Play”, time to get in place (for example to film) before the minecarts leave. The first note comes after this wait plus the minecarts' trip (shown just below). Can also be changed in the game: /function music:delay {s:5}.
  • Rails after the line — distance to the lava.
  • Floor, Resting block of the arch, Flash duration, Track colours.
  • Button console, Signs with the lane names, Who hears the music (everyone, or players near the structure).

Optimisation

Choose a profile: Best quality, Balanced or Performance. Custom (full edition) unlocks every setting: maximum active minecarts, minecart density, simultaneous notes per tick… The minimum gap between minecarts (0.5 block by default) can be set in every edition: at 0, every note has its minecart, even in very fast passages. If some notes have no minecart, the step says so and why. The Estimates panel shows the size, the number of blocks, the minecarts and the estimated server load in real time.

Step 9 — 3D preview and position (wizard steps 8 and 9)

3D preview (step 8)

  • Left click + drag: rotate. Right click + drag: move. Wheel: zoom. Double click: default view.
  • 3D view / Top / Side: preset views. Layers can be hidden (minecarts, zones, labels).
  • Animate replays the real schedule: minecarts and flashes exactly as the datapack will produce them.

Position (step 9)

By default there is nothing to set here: the place is chosen in the game, with the placement wand, after the installation (see the “In Minecraft” guide). Right-click a block: it becomes the front left corner of the structure, the zone is shown in translucent blue, nothing is placed before /function music:<name>/build. The step reminds you of your song's command and of the size of the structure.

Fixed coordinates (optional)

Choose Fixed coordinates, chosen here in the Place list to give the position from the software (for example for a server):

  1. X / Y / Z is the front corner of the structure, on the console side, where you stand. The structure extends behind it.
  2. The minecarts travel towards — the direction the minecarts move (towards you).
  3. In front of the player (last save) places the console 2 blocks in front of your last saved position, centred on you.
  4. Place on the ground sets Y just above the highest ground under the structure.
  5. The World view map (read-only) shows the footprint in blue and your last position in yellow. The number of existing blocks that would be replaced is shown.
  6. Click Confirm this position.

The position is then saved in the datapack: /function music:<name>/build builds straight away. The wand can still move it.

Tip: close Minecraft before opening this step to see an up-to-date map.

Step 10 — Generate, install and check (wizard steps 10 and 11)

Generation (step 10)

  1. The Checks list must be green: world closed in Minecraft, structure and samples ready, place (in the game with the wand, or position confirmed).

    The pack name gives the name of the song in the game: “Miss You” → /function music:miss_you/….

  2. Packs and options — pack name, description, version, icon.
  3. Click Generate and install in the world….
  4. A confirmation window lists exactly what will happen: files written, size of the structure, other songs of the world, world backup. Confirm.
  5. The software makes a ZIP backup of the world, then installs:
    • the song's datapack in datapacks/music_<name>.zip;
    • its sounds in mmstudio_packs/<name>.zip;
    • the world's resource pack (resources.zip in 1.21, resourcepacks/resources.zip in 26.x), which gathers the sounds of all the songs installed in that world: Minecraft loads it automatically when the world opens, and only in that world.

To put several songs in the same world (full edition; the free edition keeps a single one), generate and install each project one after the other: the songs already installed are kept (the Songs installed in this world line lists them). Remove a song from the world… takes its datapack and its sounds out (they are moved to the backups); remove its structure in the game first with /function music:<name>/remove.

No file is ever deleted: a replaced file is moved into the backup folder.

Generate the files only writes nothing into the world (for a server or a manual installation).

Check (step 11)

Click Check: the files are re-read exactly as the game will read them (every sound event exists and is a valid OGG Vorbis file, every sound used by the datapack is in the resource pack, the installed datapack is identical and the world pack holds the song's sounds). The song's commands are listed below.

Step 11 — In Minecraft

Goal: choose the place, build the structure and play the music.

Each song has its own commands, from the pack name: “Miss You” becomes music:miss_you, “DDLC” becomes music:ddlc. In this guide, replace <name> with the one of your song (shown in step 10 of the software and in the chat when the world opens).

  1. Start Minecraft and open the world. Its resource pack is loaded by itself — nothing to enable in the packs menu.
  2. In the chat, click [Take the wand], or type (you must be an operator / have cheats enabled):

    /function music:<name>/wand

  1. Place a block as a marker (dirt, for example), look at it and right-click with the wand. That block becomes the front left corner of the structure: it extends in front of you and to the right, and the minecarts will come towards you. The floor of the structure replaces the layer of the targeted block.
  2. While you hold the wand, the zone it takes is shown in translucent blue, with a “front of the structure” marker on the console side. Nothing is placed or broken.
    • Switch items: the zone disappears (it comes back when you take the wand again).
    • Right-click another block: the zone moves, the old one disappears.
  3. Click [Build here] in the chat, or type:

    /function music:<name>/build The chunks are loaded, then the structure is built.

  4. Click [Start the music], press the Play button of the console, or type:

    /function music:<name>/play

  1. The first note comes after the wait before the start (3 seconds by default) plus the minecarts' trip from the far end of the rails: time to get in place, for example to film. The chat says in how many seconds.
If you chose fixed coordinates in step 9 of the software, the place is already saved: go straight to step 5. The wand is only needed to change it.

To move a structure already built: /function music:<name>/remove (it is replaced with air), then a new right-click with the wand.

Several songs in the same world (full edition)

With the full edition, install as many songs as you like in the same world (step 10 of the software, one at a time): each one has its datapack, its commands and its structure, and the world's resource pack holds the sounds of all of them.

  • /function music:list: the clickable list of the songs of the world (play, stop, wand, help).
  • /function music:stop_all: stops every song.
  • Several songs can play at the same time.

The free edition keeps one song per world: installing another one takes the previous one out (it is kept in the backups).

The button console in front of the structure does the same: play, pause, stop, volume, tempo, pitch.

A literal /play music command is impossible in vanilla Minecraft without mods: the music is controlled with datapack functions. Minecraft does not accept capitals or spaces in their names either: hence music:miss_you/play.

Step 12 — Free and full editions, license

Free edition

Without a license the software is the free edition. Everything works, within these limits:

  • Songs: the first 20 seconds.
  • Up to 3 tracks and 300 notes played.
  • 8 basic instruments (no pling, bell, chime, flute, guitar, xylophones or bit).
  • Mob sounds: only the voices of 3 farm animals.
  • No custom sounds, custom optimisation profile, server export or audio export (MP3…).
  • One song per world (installing another song replaces the first one).
  • A credit message in the chat every 20 seconds while the music plays (“Music played with Minecraft MIDI Studio, made by Akatsudo”).
  • In the game, the music cannot be slowed down below its original speed.

Buying the full edition

The full edition is tied to your Google account. A key only works with the account it was bought for: even copied, it is useless to anyone else.

  1. Click Buy the full edition (License page): you are taken to the shop.
  2. Pay with your Google (Gmail) address, or write it in the order message if you pay with another address.
  3. You receive a license key by e-mail. It starts with MMS-.

Activating the key

  1. Open the License page and paste the whole key.
  2. Click Activate with Google. The software checks the key, then your browser opens Google's official sign-in page.
  3. Sign in with the Google account shown, then accept. The page says “Signed in”: go back to the software.

The full edition is unlocked immediately, without restarting. Afterwards the software works without internet on this computer.

Changing computer

The key is kept in a hidden folder of your Google Drive reserved for the software (it does not appear among your files). On another computer, on the License page, click Get my license back with Google and sign in: that's all, no key needed.

Remove the license from this computer removes the activation from this computer only; the license stays in your Google account.

Privacy

  • You sign in on Google's page, in your browser: the software never sees your password.
  • It only receives your e-mail address (to check that the key is yours) and access to the software's hidden folder in your Google Drive, where the key is kept. It cannot see your other files.
  • Nothing is sent to the author or anywhere else. On the computer, the activation is stored encrypted.

Step 13 — Playing on a server

Goal: use the music on a multiplayer server (full edition).

  1. In step 11, click Export for a server… and choose a folder. You get:
    • music_<name>.zip (the song's datapack);
    • the resource pack (.zip);
    • README-SERVER.txt: the instructions, with the pack's SHA-1.
  2. Datapack: copy music_<name>.zip into <server world>/datapacks/, then type /reload (or restart the server).
  3. Resource pack: put the resource pack at a direct download address (HTTP or HTTPS), then in server.properties:
    resource-pack=<address of the file>
resource-pack-sha1=<SHA-1 given in the instructions>
require-resource-pack=true   (optional)
  1. For the button console: enable-command-block=true in server.properties.
  2. In game, as an operator: /function music:<name>/wand for the placement wand (right-click a block = front left corner of the structure, zone shown in blue), then /function music:<name>/build and /function music:<name>/play. With fixed coordinates chosen in the software, the wand is not needed.

Players who are not operators control the music with /trigger music.<name>.play, /trigger music.<name>.volume set 150, etc.

Several songs on the server: copy each datapack into datapacks/. The server accepts one resource pack: install the songs in a world with the software (step 10), then host the resources.zip of that world, which holds the sounds of all of them (compute its SHA-1 for example with certutil -hashfile resources.zip SHA1).

The sounds of the pack are Minecraft's (they belong to Mojang): use them for your server, but do not publish the pack.

Step 14 — Troubleshooting

No sound in game

  1. Did you open the world after installing? The world's resource pack is loaded when the world opens.
  2. Watch for a pack error message when the world opens. Run Check (step 11) again: it must say Check passed.
  3. Check the game's Jukebox/Note Blocks volume slider (Options → Music & Sounds).
  4. Try /function music:<name>/volume {v:150}.
  5. In the log of Minecraft (.minecraft\logs\latest.log), a line “Unable to play unknown soundEvent” means the resource pack is not loaded: generate and install again.

The structure is not built

  • Commands must be allowed (operator / cheats).
  • Choose the place first: /function music:<name>/wand, then right-click a block with the wand.
  • If the area could not be loaded in 30 s, nothing is changed: try again.

The wand does nothing

  • Hold it in your main hand and aim at a block less than 64 blocks away (the sky or water alone do not count).
  • A right-click on a block that opens (chest, door, lever…) uses that block instead of the wand: aim at an ordinary block.
  • Each song has its own wand: take the one of the right song (/function music:<name>/wand).
  • A structure already built does not move: /function music:<name>/remove first.

“Official Minecraft sounds not downloaded”

The first download (about 41 MB from Mojang's servers) could not be done, usually because there is no internet. Check the connection, then Settings → Official Minecraft sounds → Download now. An antivirus or a company firewall may also block piston-meta.mojang.com and resources.download.minecraft.net.

The license does not activate

  • “This key was bought for the Google account …”: sign in with the Google account shown (its first letter and domain are displayed). If you paid with another address, ask the author for a key for your Google address.
  • “No license is stored in the Google account”: use Activate with Google with the key received by e-mail (the first time); then Get my license back works on your other computers.
  • Google's page does not come back to the software: keep the software open while signing in, and let your firewall accept the local connection (address 127.0.0.1).
  • “This activation comes from another computer”: normal after copying your settings; click Get my license back with Google.

A world does not appear in the list

  • It appears by itself after a moment; Refresh the list forces a search.
  • For an unusual game folder: Add an instance folder… (step 1) or Settings → Added instances.

The software shows an error

Open Diagnostics: it shows the detected environment, runs a self-test and shows the log. Copy the report or Export the report… to share it when you ask for help. Nothing is ever sent automatically.

Undo everything in a world

  • In game, for each song: /function music:<name>/remove (removes the structure), then /function music:<name>/uninstall.
  • Then, in the software (step 10): Remove a song from the world…. Or by hand: delete datapacks/music_<name>.zip, the mmstudio_packs folder and the world's resources.zip (or resourcepacks/resources.zip).
  • Or restore the ZIP backup made before the installation.
