Step 6 — Instruments and drums (wizard step 4)

Goal: give each track a Minecraft sound.

  1. Each track automatically gets a sound from its General MIDI instrument number, with a fixed table of the 128 instruments, like Note Block Studio's MIDI import (see the table at the bottom of this guide): for example no. 1 piano → harp, no. 33 to 40 (basses) → double bass, no. 41 violin → flute. The track name and its notes do not matter. The reason is written under the sound.
  2. To change it: choose another sound in the catalog on the right (search box, categories, Listen), then click Assign the selected sound to the track.
  3. Adjust the Relative volume and the Transposition (in semitones) of the track.
  4. Lowest note / Highest note let you hear the extremes of the track with the chosen sound.

Drum tracks

A percussion track uses a key → sound table: each drum key (kick, snare, hi-hat…) is linked to a sound and a transposition, the way Note Block Studio creators do it. Keys without a sound can be ignored or played with the snare drum. Restore the default table undoes your changes.

Sounds available

  • Note blocks (official Minecraft files: those of your installation, or downloaded from Mojang).
  • Mob sounds of the selected Minecraft version, sorted by category (voice, hurt, movement…).
  • Custom sounds placed in the Note Block Studio Custom folder (full edition, if Note Block Studio is installed).
Free edition: 8 basic note block instruments (no pling, bell, chime, flute, guitar, xylophones or bit), and only the voices of 3 farm animals for mob sounds.
